What is an ESTA?

The Electronic System for Travel Authorisation (ESTA) is a mandatory pre-travel requirement for nationals of Visa Waiver Programme (VWP) countries travelling to the United States by air or sea. It is not a visa — it is a travel authorisation that determines whether you are eligible to travel to the US under the Visa Waiver Programme.

An approved ESTA is valid for two years or until your passport expires, whichever comes first, and allows multiple trips to the United States during that period, with each stay limited to 90 days.

Who needs an ESTA?

You need an ESTA if you hold a passport from a Visa Waiver Programme country and are travelling to the United States for tourism, business, medical treatment, or transit by air or sea.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an ESTA if I am just transiting through the USA?
Yes. Even if you are transiting through a US airport without leaving the secure area, you still require a valid ESTA or US visa if you hold a VWP-eligible passport.
Can I apply for an ESTA for my whole family?
Yes. Each traveller requires their own individual ESTA linked to their own passport. ETA Permits accepts family applications and reviews all submissions together.
My ESTA was approved but I have a new passport. Do I need a new one?
Yes. Your ESTA is linked to your passport number. If you have renewed your passport you must apply for a new ESTA, even if your previous one has not expired.
How long does ESTA approval take?
Most applications receive a decision within 72 hours. In some cases approval may come within minutes. We recommend applying at least 72 hours before your departure.
What is the difference between an ESTA and a US visa?
An ESTA is a travel authorisation for short stays under the Visa Waiver Programme, available to nationals of participating countries. A US visa is required for those who do not qualify for the VWP, or who wish to stay longer than 90 days, study, or work in the US.
Is my ESTA valid for multiple visits?
Yes. An approved ESTA allows multiple trips to the United States within its two-year validity period, with each visit limited to 90 days.
